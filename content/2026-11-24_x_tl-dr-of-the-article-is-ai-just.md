tl;dr of the article is AI just solved the riemann hypothesis while no human could, and it's way harder than most of our industries' problems, so we have superintelligence

tbh i don't buy this, and here's why - 

LLMs we have today are trained through two stages: pre-training and post-training. if you want a simple mental model - pre-training teaches wisdom, and post-training teaches behaviors

solving the riemann hypothesis is proof that our pre-training is already quite incredible, although i think even there we still have a lot more to do in terms of multi-modality and physical world modeling

post-training however is quite a mess. i explained in many of my previous tweets the limitations of the current RLVR pipelines. it's why the same AI that can solve riemann hypothesis would still produce useless comments, bad test cases, and slop writing etc etc

what we have is something highly intelligent in theory, but still suck at many real world tasks, because the behaviors required to do well in those tasks had to be taught one by one

agentic coding, browser use, computer use, 3d modeling - each skill had to be trained in the labs through predefined RL environments. the models can't really learn these skills on the fly like how a 16yr-old human can learn how to drive with just a few days

it doesn't look like we already have these fundamental problems figured out yet. and it's unclear that being able to solve the riemann hypothesis proves there are viable solutions to these unknowns

all in all, i think what we have is already super exciting, but it's still early to say we have superintelligence figured out. what do you think? do you feel you have superintelligence at hand?
