### @kunchenguid

over the past couple of weeks, i heard multiple people telling me they could no longer use up their LLM subscriptions

the reasons were a mix of:
1. the frontier models became more efficient 
2. they felt exhausted managing agents
3. they ran out of problems that they can throw to AI

1 will continue to happen. 2 is a tooling and workflow problem (just use firstmate haha) that will be solved as well

3 is an interesting one - it points at a novelty effect that may be wearing off, and a capability gap that AI just hasn’t reached the level that most people can trust to run their daily lives yet

meanwhile, my quotas have never been tighter because i have an endless number of things i want to build

that points at the reality of user segmentation - R&D is such a niche use case that needs an astounding amount of tokens. there aren’t many other use cases that are so token intensive 

realistically, how many % of the world’s population will want to run an army of agents and build software all day? i don’t expect that to ever become a big number

so my speculation is that the current $200 subscriptions are likely already close to the ceiling of most people’s consumption needs. better models will unlock more use cases, but efficiency gains may cancel that out

like water, gas, power - most households will simply get one or two monthly AI subscription and be done

now the important part - this means if you are building a B2C business, you should almost never sell tokens as part of your offering, because there will be no demand. people already have their subscriptions and that’s enough

instead, allow people to bring their own LLM subscriptions to your product, and add value on top. i suspect this will hold for most B2B businesses as well, but for B2C this is almost certain at this point
