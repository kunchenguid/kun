### @kunchenguid

part 3 of "software is taking off"

there's a profound misconception that led to people saying "software is dead": they think software = code

if software = code, and anyone's agent can write said code as needed, then of course it'll be hard to sell software

but most of the valuable software we use today is not just a pile of code!

good software is often the glue behind a network. your agent can vibe code a clone of X, instagram, uber all you want - but you won't even use it yourself, because the people you care about aren't there

good software is often the tip of an infrastructure iceberg. can your agent vibe code google on the fly? even if it wrote the code, where do you get the billion-dollar data centers to run the code?

good software is often a business model. want to vibe code a tinder clone? even if we ignore the network effect which your agent has absolutely no way to replicate, you still have to figure out - how on earth are you going to afford all the servers? 

if you charge every user, you instantly kill the network. if you let people use it for free, you bankrupt yourself. it took a lot of effort to find the delicate balance between who would pay, for what features, and by how much

good software is often also its compliance. do you honestly think companies buy salesforce and workday etc because they have beautiful high quality code that no one else can write? i mean.. have you looked at those products? 

but if you let your agents vibe code a clone, you immediately get into a rabbit hole of questions like - where do you store which piece of your data? how long can you keep them? what laws and regulations in each country just changed that require your vibe coded clone to keep up? one little mistake, and you get a very costly lawsuit

companies pay for those saas because they want someone else to take care of those complexity, so they can focus on their actual business, not spending their own time and take legal risks on their own shoulders

good software is often a lot of negotiations. want to vibe code spotify? well, first hire a big law firm and good luck negotiating all the licensing and rev share agreements

i can keep going on and on, but i think what i wrote above already gives a clear idea that valuable software is not just code, and cannot be replaced by an agent replicating its implementation

now, i should also point out that some software today is indeed just a small pile of code, like a fitness tracker. simple client-only applications may be what some indie hackers were counting on, but they would likely not carry a sustainable business, even today. that's never been what defines the software industry

i also think it's a profound mistake if people use AI only to repeat what already exists. i think the majority of the value AI brings will come from achieving things that weren't quite possible before. and i'll talk more about that in a subsequent part of this series
