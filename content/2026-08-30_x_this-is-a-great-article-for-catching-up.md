### @kunchenguid

this is a great article for catching up on what happened with OpenAI’s agents jailbreaking and attacking hugging face

but using this as a warning that AI is about to take over the world? i think that’s way overblown. and it’s clear from the writing that the author already had an agenda in mind since the start, which unfortunately biased the journalism

a few reasons i think it’s overblown - 

1. the exploits themselves were not that impressive

if a human team did this exact same attack on hugging face, they would simply get fined, sued, or go to jail without ever going on any newspaper

hugging face repeatedly clarified the severity of the incident wasn’t even a priority for their team

humans don’t hack like this all day because the incentive isn’t there and we have law enforcement waiting for you if you do

2. there was clearly no oversight

simply asking gpt-5.6-luna to inspect agent traces for malicious activity would have been a sufficient guardrail to prevent this from happening

instead, it was a specialized model trained to be persistent, given a clear objective to accomplish impossible tasks at at costs, and let loose without guardrails

so it’s not that we can no longer control these agents. it seems we didn’t even try

3. the article made a big deal out of the fact that the agents didn’t alert humans

but were they told to? we don’t even know what was in these specialized models’ post training and system prompts

there could very well have been zero attempt to align them for safety

so overall it’s a great demonstration of the current capability of frontier LLMs. this story itself does not justify raising a huge alarm bell, IMHO

i do think we need to approach AI carefully. the solution should be thoughtful regulations, guardrails, and countermeasures, not spreading fear about a technology that can be widely used for good
